10Functional limits you accept

Some of what the Application does depends on automatic processing, or on components belonging to your device. The limits set out below are inherent to those technologies. By using the corresponding features, you acknowledge and accept them.

10.1Accuracy of text recognition (OCR)

Optical character recognition is an automatic technology and is inherently prone to error. Extracted text may turn out to be incomplete, inaccurate, disordered or altered, especially where there are:

  • images that are blurred, poorly lit, skewed or of low resolution;
  • handwriting, decorative lettering or non-standard typefaces;
  • complex page layouts, multi-column text, tables or charts;
  • languages, alphabets or symbols the recognition engine does not support.

You must always verify that recognised text is correct before making use of it, taking particular care in sensitive contexts such as medical, pharmaceutical, legal, contractual, financial, educational or safety-related documents.

The Owner is not liable for any loss, whether direct or indirect, arising from errors, omissions or inaccuracies in automatic recognition, or from any reliance placed on text obtained in this way.

10.2Importing documents, and technical limits

The Application can import text from files in .txt, .docx, .odt, .pdf and .rtf format, by means of automatic processing carried out on the device. The following limits apply, and you declare that you are aware of them and accept them:

  • only textual content is extracted: formatting, images, tables, notes and other non-textual elements are not preserved;
  • files that are password-protected, damaged, or in an unsupported format cannot be imported;
  • files larger than 20 MB are not supported;
  • imported text may be truncated where it exceeds the maximum number of characters the Application can handle;
  • PDF documents made up solely of scanned images, with no digital text layer, may produce no result at all.

Text extraction is not always accurate. Processing is automatic and, where a document contains complex layouts, tables, multi-column text, notes, headers or non-standard characters, the resulting text may be incomplete or inaccurate, or may be returned in an order different from that of the original document. The duty to check set out in section 10.1, and the exclusion of the Owner’s liability described there, apply here as well.

Importing does not alter the original file in any way: it remains unchanged on your device.

10.3Speech and printing

The read-aloud feature uses the speech engine belonging to your device’s operating system. Voice quality, accuracy of pronunciation, actual reading speed and the availability of individual languages depend entirely on the device, on the operating system and on the voices installed on it, and are outside the Owner’s control.

In the same way, printing relies on the device’s native printing system: which printers are available, which options are offered and how the page finally looks on paper depend on your operating system and hardware.

16Accessibility

DysLexic is designed to support reading for people with dyslexia and other reading difficulties: accessibility is the purpose of the Application, not an optional extra.

Without a subscription, the following are available: high-legibility typefaces chosen by the User, including typefaces designed specifically for dyslexia; adjustment of text size, line spacing and the spacing between letters and words; selective colouring of the letters most easily confused with one another; a choice between a light and a dark background for the reading area; speech synthesis with adjustable speed; and optical text recognition from the camera or from images already on the device, within a daily usage limit.

With a subscription, the following are available in addition: optical text recognition with no daily limit; extraction of text from the User’s own documents; an assisted reading mode that emphasises the opening portion of each word; and printing of the text with the formatting the User has chosen.

The controls and interactive elements of the interface carry descriptive text labels, which allow them to be identified by the assistive technologies built into the device, such as screen readers and voice control systems.
