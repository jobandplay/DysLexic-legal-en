Privacy Policy

What DysLexic does with information, which parts of it never leave your device, which parts do — and what you can ask for.

Last updated: 9 October 2026 Version: 2.2 Application: DysLexic (com.kimboj.dyslexic)

DysLexic helps people with dyslexia read. It was built around one commitment: the material you bring to it — your texts, your photographs, your documents — is processed by your own device and goes nowhere else. There is no account to create and no email address to hand over.

One part of the app does have to reach the outside world, and that is the part that manages the Pro subscription. This notice keeps the two apart on purpose, because the difference is fundamental: what you read with DysLexic stays yours and stays on the phone, while what concerns your purchase is handled by outside providers, each of them named openly below.

In short

  • Your texts, photos and documents are read and processed on the device. The Owner never receives them, never stores them, and is not technically able to see them.
  • Text recognition runs on the device: the picture of your page is not uploaded to Google or to anyone else.
  • The only data that leaves the device is what the Pro subscription needs: a random identifier, the state of your purchase and some technical details, handled by the provider RevenueCat.
  • There is no advertising, no profiling, no analytics of your behaviour and no sale of data to anybody.

This box is a plain-language summary, offered for convenience. The sections that follow are the notice itself.

2How the app is built, and why it matters

Most of this notice follows from one architectural fact, so it is worth setting out first.

The heart of the app runs entirely on your device. Photograph a page and the image is examined by your phone's own processor. Import a PDF and the file is opened and read on the phone. Change a typeface or a colour and the preference is written into the app's own storage. At no point in any of this does the content travel to a server belonging to the Owner — for the straightforward reason that no such server exists. The Owner operates no infrastructure capable of receiving your texts.

Around that core there are two points of contact with the outside world, each described in full further on:

  • text recognition, which uses a Google library that works on the device itself (section 5);
  • the Pro subscription, which is handled by RevenueCat together with the app stores (section 7).

Only the second involves sending data to a provider working on the Owner's behalf. The first concerns a component of the platform itself, and it does not transmit your content.

4Device permissions

Several features cannot work unless the app is allowed to reach a specific part of your device. Each permission you grant is an act of authorisation on your part: it opens a door onto material already held on your device. This section says which doors exist and how far each one opens; the sections that follow explain what is then done behind them.

Nothing is requested up front. Each permission is asked for at the moment its feature is first used, and every one of them can be withdrawn later from your operating system's settings.

Camera — used without recording and without saving

The camera lets you frame printed text and capture a single still to be turned into text. The preview stays on screen, the captured frame is analysed and then discarded: nothing is written to your photo gallery and no video is produced.

DysLexic does not record audio and does not touch the microphone. The audio-recording permission is explicitly disabled in the app's configuration — RECORD_AUDIO is listed among the blocked permissions — so the operating system will not grant it even if something were to ask.

Photos and images — one file at a time

This is only needed if you choose to run recognition on a picture already saved on your device. The app receives the single image you select, not access to your gallery as a whole, and it does not copy that image anywhere.

Documents and device storage

Importing a document goes through the system file picker: you choose the file, and the app receives that file and nothing else. DysLexic does not request a general storage permission — writing to external storage is expressly blocked in the app's configuration, where WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE is listed among the blocked permissions. The app therefore cannot browse, alter or delete other files on your device. Anything the app produces for printing or exporting is written into its own private area.

Network access

The internet connection is used solely for managing the subscription and for the technical exchanges described in section 5. Reading, recognition and importing all work without a connection.

If you refuse a permission

The app carries on working. The only thing you lose is the feature tied to that permission: without the camera you can still paste text, import documents and use every reading feature. Permissions can be revoked at any time from your operating system's settings.

5Text recognition: Google ML Kit

Converting an image into editable text — optical character recognition, or OCR — is carried out by Google ML Kit Text Recognition, a library supplied by Google LLC and built into the app.

The key point

Your image is not uploaded anywhere. ML Kit works in on-device mode: the recognition model runs on your own hardware. In Google's own words, processing of the input data happens fully on-device and “ML Kit does not send that data and the resultant outputs to Google servers”. Neither the photograph of your page nor the text read from it reaches Google. This is exactly why the library was chosen.

ML Kit is nonetheless a proprietary Google component, and two things about it should be stated plainly:

  • Google receives measurement data about the library itself. Google's ML Kit terms state that the APIs “send metrics about the performance and utilization of the APIs in your app to Google”, which Google uses for measurement, debugging, maintenance, improvement and the detection of misuse. This is telemetry about how the component behaves — not the content of your images or of the recognised text.
  • The library contacts Google from time to time. Google states that the ML Kit APIs “may contact Google servers from time to time in order to receive things like bug fixes, updated models and hardware accelerator compatibility information”. On Android these exchanges, including the download and updating of the recognition models, are handled by the operating system and by Google Play services rather than by the app, and they are governed by Google's own privacy policy.

6Printing, exporting and the clipboard

Printing. When you print, the app lays out a formatted page and hands it to your operating system's printing service. From that moment the job belongs to the system and to the printer you pick: if you use a cloud printing service, that service's own terms apply.

Exporting and sharing. The app can turn your formatted text into a PDF and pass it either to the system sharing sheet or to a folder you choose. Once you select a destination — an email client, a messaging app, a cloud drive, a local folder — the file is in the hands of whatever you selected, under its rules and not the app's. DysLexic never sends that file anywhere on its own initiative.

Clipboard. Copying and pasting happen only when you explicitly ask for them. Bear in mind that the system clipboard is a shared resource: other installed apps may, within the limits the operating system sets, read what is on it. That is a characteristic of the platform, not of DysLexic.

7The Pro subscription and RevenueCat

DysLexic offers a Pro subscription that unlocks additional features. To administer it, the app uses RevenueCat, Inc., a company based in the United States that provides subscription-management infrastructure.

What role RevenueCat plays

RevenueCat acts as a processor within the meaning of Article 28 of the GDPR. Its own data processing addendum records the arrangement explicitly: the customer — here, the Owner — is the controller, and RevenueCat is the processor. It handles data solely on the Owner's behalf and on the Owner's documented instructions, and may not use it for purposes of its own. Decisions about what data is processed, and why, remain the Owner's alone.

What it actually does

When you start or restore a subscription, the app asks RevenueCat whether you are entitled to the Pro features. To answer, RevenueCat receives and stores the items listed in table 3.2: a random identifier standing in for your subscriber profile, the state and history of your purchases, the proof of purchase issued by the store, some technical details about the device, and the IP address of the connection. RevenueCat verifies receipts with Google Play or the App Store and returns a single plain answer to the app: subscription active, or not.

The service also lets the Owner look at how subscriptions are performing in aggregate — activations, renewals and cancellations. Those figures cannot be traced back to individual people.

RevenueCat stores this data in Amazon Web Services data centres located in the United States, and traffic to it is encrypted in transit.

What does not happen

  • Payment details pass through neither DysLexic nor RevenueCat. Card, account and billing information are handled exclusively by Google Play or the App Store.
  • RevenueCat never receives your texts, your photographs or your documents. That content never leaves the device in the first place.
  • Subscription data is not used for advertising, for profiling or for sale to third parties.
  • The subscriber identifier is a randomly generated code. It contains no name, no email address and nothing else that identifies you directly.

14Security

The most effective safeguard DysLexic applies is structural: content that is never collected cannot be breached. Since there is no central repository of users' texts, there is no breach capable of exposing them.

On the device, the app's data lives in the private area the operating system assigns to it, isolated from other applications and protected by the device's encryption where that is switched on.

Communications with RevenueCat and with the stores travel over encrypted channels (HTTPS/TLS). Subscription data is protected by the technical and organisational measures those providers apply, described in their respective notices.

To protect your own texts, we suggest keeping a screen lock enabled, keeping the operating system up to date, and installing the app only from official stores.

15Your rights, and how to use them

The GDPR gives you the rights set out in Articles 15 to 22 — access to your data, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, portability and objection — as well as the right to withdraw consent at any time.

For data on the device

Because this data stays in your possession, these rights are exercised directly in the app, with immediate effect and without having to ask anyone:

This is the data the Owner can act on, and your rights over it are exercised by writing to jobandplay.support@gmail.com. You may ask what data is associated with your subscriber profile, have it corrected or erased, have its processing restricted, receive it in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format, or object to processing founded on legitimate interest.

If the data held is genuinely insufficient to identify you, Article 11 of the GDPR applies: the Owner is not obliged to obtain additional information for the sole purpose of identifying you, will say so plainly, and will act on your request as soon as you supply details that make the record findable.

Requests are free of charge, and you will receive an answer without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt, as Article 12 of the GDPR provides. Data that must be retained by law, accounting records in particular, is unaffected.

5Riconoscimento del testo: Google ML Kit

Il punto essenziale

La tua immagine non viene caricata da nessuna parte. ML Kit lavora in modalità «on-device»: il modello di riconoscimento gira sul tuo hardware. Secondo quanto dichiara Google stessa, l’elaborazione dei dati in ingresso avviene interamente sul dispositivo e ML Kit non invia quei dati, né i risultati che ne derivano, ai server di Google. Né la fotografia della tua pagina né il testo che ne viene letto arrivano a Google. È proprio per questo che è stata scelta questa libreria.

ML Kit resta comunque un componente proprietario di Google, e due cose vanno dette con chiarezza:

  • Google riceve dati di misurazione sulla libreria stessa. I termini di ML Kit di Google dichiarano che le API inviano a Google metriche sulle prestazioni e sull’utilizzo delle API nell’app, che Google usa per la misurazione, il debug, la manutenzione, il miglioramento e il rilevamento degli usi impropri. Si tratta di telemetria sul comportamento del componente, non del contenuto delle tue immagini o del testo riconosciuto.
  • La libreria contatta Google di tanto in tanto. Google dichiara che le API di ML Kit possono contattare di tanto in tanto i server di Google per ricevere, per esempio, correzioni di errori, modelli aggiornati e informazioni sulla compatibilità degli acceleratori hardware. 4Permissões do dispositivo

Várias funções não conseguem funcionar se a aplicação não for autorizada a aceder a uma parte específica do seu dispositivo. Cada permissão que concede é um ato de autorização da sua parte: abre uma porta para material que já se encontra no seu dispositivo. Esta secção indica que portas existem e até onde cada uma se abre; as secções seguintes explicam o que é feito depois por detrás delas.

Nada é pedido antecipadamente. Cada permissão é pedida no momento em que a respetiva função é utilizada pela primeira vez, e todas podem ser retiradas mais tarde nas definições do seu sistema operativo.

Câmara — utilizada sem gravar e sem guardar

A câmara permite-lhe enquadrar texto impresso e captar uma única imagem fixa para ser transformada em texto. A pré-visualização mantém-se no ecrã, a imagem captada é analisada e depois descartada: nada é escrito na sua galeria de fotografias e não é produzido nenhum vídeo.

A DysLexic não grava áudio e não toca no microfone. A permissão de gravação de áudio está explicitamente desativada na configuração da aplicação — RECORD_AUDIO consta da lista de permissões bloqueadas —, pelo que o sistema operativo não a concederá, mesmo que algo a viesse a pedir.

Fotografias e imagens — um ficheiro de cada vez

Esta permissão só é necessária se optar por executar o reconhecimento sobre uma imagem já guardada no seu dispositivo. A aplicação recebe a única imagem que seleciona, e não acesso a toda a sua galeria, e não copia essa imagem para lado nenhum.

Documentos e armazenamento do dispositivo

A importação de um documento passa pelo seletor de ficheiros do sistema: o utilizador escolhe o ficheiro, e a aplicação recebe esse ficheiro e nada mais. A DysLexic não pede uma permissão geral de armazenamento: a escrita no armazenamento externo está expressamente bloqueada na configuração da aplicação, onde WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE consta da lista de permissões bloqueadas. A aplicação não pode, por isso, percorrer, alterar ou apagar outros ficheiros do seu dispositivo. Tudo o que a aplicação produz para imprimir ou exportar é escrito na sua própria área privada.

Acesso à rede

A ligação à Internet é utilizada unicamente para gerir a subscrição e para as trocas técnicas descritas na secção 5. A leitura, o reconhecimento e a importação funcionam todos sem ligação.

Se recusar uma permissão

A aplicação continua a funcionar. A única coisa que perde é a função ligada a essa permissão: sem a câmara, pode continuar a colar texto, a importar documentos e a utilizar todas as funções de leitura. As permissões podem ser revogadas a qualquer momento nas definições do seu sistema operativo.

5Reconhecimento de texto: Google ML Kit

A conversão de uma imagem em texto editável — o reconhecimento ótico de caracteres, ou OCR — é realizada pelo Google ML Kit Text Recognition, uma biblioteca fornecida pela Google LLC e incorporada na aplicação.

O ponto essencial

A sua imagem não é enviada para lado nenhum. O ML Kit trabalha em modo «on-device»: o modelo de reconhecimento é executado no seu próprio hardware. Segundo a própria Google, o processamento dos dados de entrada ocorre inteiramente no dispositivo e o ML Kit não envia esses dados nem os resultados obtidos para os servidores da Google. Nem a fotografia da sua página nem o texto que dela é lido chegam à Google. Foi exatamente por isso que esta biblioteca foi escolhida.

O ML Kit é, ainda assim, um componente proprietário da Google, e há duas coisas sobre ele que devem ser ditas com clareza:

  • A Google recebe dados de medição sobre a própria biblioteca. Os termos do ML Kit da Google indicam que as API enviam à Google métricas sobre o desempenho e a utilização das API na aplicação, que a Google utiliza para medição, depuração, manutenção, melhoria e deteção de utilizações indevidas. Trata-se de telemetria sobre o comportamento do componente, e não do conteúdo das suas imagens ou do texto reconhecido.
  • A biblioteca contacta a Google de tempos a tempos. A Google indica que as API do ML Kit podem contactar os servidores da Google de tempos a tempos para receber, por exemplo, correções de erros, modelos atualizados e informações sobre a compatibilidade dos aceleradores de hardware. No Android, estas trocas, incluindo o descarregamento e a atualização dos modelos de reconhecimento, são geridas pelo sistema operativo e pelos Serviços do Google Play, e não pela aplicação, e regem-se pela política de privacidade da própria Google.
